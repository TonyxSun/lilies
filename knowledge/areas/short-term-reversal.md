# Short-term reversal / continuation (1–4 weeks) — worker B, cycle 12 (2026-10-22 ~12:45 ET)
Cross-refs: T-003 (does not survive costs), BP-003 (LLM extrapolation), S-R5 (X spikes).

## Key findings
- In current mega caps, last week's losers beat last week's winners by just +0.18% over the next 10 days. That is under 1 standard error and a 52.6% hit rate (ST-001). There is no edge at our horizon in either direction.
- Big single-day moves of ±5% are followed by coin-flip 10-day excess returns. Medians: -0.12% after up days, +0.37% after down days (ST-002).
- Reversal is real in the broad, small and illiquid cross-section, but it is pay for providing liquidity and is eaten by costs (ST-003, T-003).

## Evidence strength
- The literature is A.
- The own test is B with caveats: survivorship (the current top 30), overlapping windows, and a single sample from 2015 to 2026.

## What doesn't apply
- Small-cap reversal strategies: our universe is mega caps and liquid ETFs, and $1k with costs leaves no room for them.
- VIX-spike reversal harvesting (Nagel): there is no high-VIX regime right now (verify with Q/R workers before relying on this).

## Rule candidates
- **ST-R1:** Don't use "it's up a lot this week" or "it's down a lot this week" as a reason to buy a mega cap. A trend-chasing or dip-buying rationale must be backed by a pre-registered trigger from another area (ST-001/002, BP-R1).
- **ST-R2:** After a ±5% day in a name we hold, don't react mechanically. The 10-day outcome is a coin flip, so re-check the thesis rather than the price (ST-002).
- **ST-R3:** If a reviewer or LLM forecast cites "momentum" at a ≤1-month horizon, discount it to zero. Momentum is a 3–12 month effect and short-horizon is reversal territory (ST-003, BP-003).
