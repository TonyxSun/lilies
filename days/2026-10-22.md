## Decisions and why

### 1. Use the FriesTrader shape: research after the close, verify the next morning, mechanical risk rules
- **Source.** The Xiaohongshu post by 抢走所有人的薯条 (physics PhD, FriesTrader developer), about 2026-08-07, with 3,379 likes and 162 comments.
- **What it does.** After the close, Claude writes theses with sources and confidence and places no orders. The next morning it re-verifies them, then runs position caps, stops, a daily loss limit and wash-sale checks. The AI can't override these. Everything is logged, and new users paper trade first.
- **Its track record.** The author's later posts report $500 at the start and a +2.2% first week. By month three the account was $1,538.82, up 2.33% that month, while QQQ fell 1.05% and VOO fell 0.01% (screenshots in the author's 2026-10-19 post). The week-8 post reported +1.62% against QQQ -0.15% (2026-09-18).
- **Why we picked it.** It is the only one of the three with a real process. It separates thinking from acting, has hard limits, keeps a log and starts on paper. A commenter (Hensen) also said the overnight re-check saved them from reversals.
- **Caveat.** The returns are small, short-run and self-reported, and the author says the approach can't be backtested.
